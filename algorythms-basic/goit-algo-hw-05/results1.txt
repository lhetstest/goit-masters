───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CONCLUSIONS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Boyer-Moore:
  Performs best on long patterns with a large alphabet (e.g. English/Ukrainian
  text). The bad-character heuristic lets it skip large portions of the text,
  making it sub-linear on average. It tends to be the fastest for real-world
  text search when the pattern is not trivially short.

Knuth-Morris-Pratt (KMP):
  Guarantees O(n + m) worst-case time regardless of the pattern or alphabet.
  The failure function avoids re-examining characters, so it is consistent and
  predictable. On short or repetitive patterns it can outperform Boyer-Moore.
  Good choice when worst-case guarantees matter more than average speed.

Rabin-Karp:
  Uses rolling hashing; good when searching for multiple patterns simultaneously.
  For a single pattern it is usually the slowest of the three because of the
  hashing overhead and potential hash collisions requiring character-by-character
  verification. Suitable for plagiarism detection / multi-pattern problems.

★ RECOMMENDATION: 'Boyer-Moore' showed the lowest total execution time
  across both articles and both pattern types in this benchmark.
  For general Ukrainian/English text search, Boyer-Moore is typically the
  fastest practical choice due to its large-skip heuristic.
